## 9. My assessment

**Risks**

- **HIGH_RISK is a concentrated bet on Nordic financials, not a diversified high-risk portfolio.** DNB Bank (DNB.OL) and Storebrand (STB.OL) alone are 34 %, and with Investor B (INVE-B.ST) financials are 49 %. These three will fall together on any Norwegian or Swedish rate or credit shock. Norges Bank and the Riksbank are both due to make rate decisions in December. The portfolio also holds no US stocks at all, although its own agents rank US names highest.
- **MAIN has two clusters that move together:**
  - financials at 28.4 %, across five names in four countries but all rate-driven
  - oil at 11 %: Var Energi (VAR.OL) and Exxon Mobil (XOM), with a correlation of 0.59

  Var Energi (VAR.OL) is already the worst holding at −4.0 %. An OPEC+ decision or a further fall in the oil price would hit both at once.
- **The defensive and momentum theme is crowded.** Coca-Cola (KO), Costco (COST), Iberdrola (IBE.MC) and AbbVie (ABBV) were all bought while the value agent flagged them as stretched against their 5-year history. If the market rotates back into tech and growth, these are the names that tend to lag. Both portfolios would then trail iShares MSCI ACWI ETF (ACWI) together.
- **Currency is invisible.** 72 % of MAIN and 66 % of HIGH_RISK is in foreign currency. The purchase-FX rule is a legitimate choice for counting realised results. But it makes the headline value overstate both portfolios this week, and it makes any comparison with iShares MSCI ACWI ETF (ACWI) or SPDR S&P 500 ETF (SPY) (both priced in USD) misleading in either direction.
- **Valuation timing.** Nordic and European holdings are valued at their 10:20–10:40 prices, and US holdings at 16:45. The day-end value, and so this week's numbers, are a midday snapshot, not a close.

**Costs and turnover:** fine for a build-up week. The risk is next week, when the first rotations become possible: HIGH_RISK from 28 November, and MAIN from 3 December.

**Suggestions (none applied):**

1. **Fix the adverse selection in the sector and region caps.** When a candidate fails the cap check, either shrink it to fit the room that is left, or stop considering the whole sector for that session. Today the cap blocks Allianz (ALV.DE) at +0.38 but admits ING Group (INGA.AS) at +0.32, only because ING's target is smaller. The exposure counter also adds the *target* weight rather than the actual fill, so the bookkeeping slowly drifts away from the real weights.
2. **MAIN: either allow more slots, or require a minimum position weight.** The portfolio is full at 15 positions with 9.6 % cash, 4.6 points above the 5 % reserve. Several slots hold small positions, such as Zurich Insurance (ZURN.SW) at 3.7 % and ING Group (INGA.AS) at 4.3 %. Options: raise `max_positions` to about 18; let a full portfolio top up existing holdings that are still rated "buy"; or don't open positions below about 4 %.
3. **HIGH_RISK: allow rotation across regions** (still open from the daily reviews). The US session can never rotate, because HIGH_RISK has no US holdings. If the weakest holding in any region could be sold at its last price, AbbVie (ABBV) at +0.59 could replace a Nordic laggard.
4. **Session budget combined with the minimum fill rule.** With 34 % of the cash for the Nordic session and "at least 50 % of the target", any Nordic buy fails once deployable cash drops below about 1.5 times the target. One option is to measure the 50 % against what the session is allowed to spend, or to give unused Nordic budget back to later sessions. Otherwise MAIN's Nordic share will only shrink.
5. **Report both FX views in the headline numbers.** This doesn't mean changing the owner's rule. Put the value at today's FX rate next to the official value in the daily summary and the dashboard header, and use it for the benchmark comparison. Alternatively, also compare with iShares MSCI ACWI ETF (ACWI) converted to NOK.
6. **Record a proper starting point for the benchmarks.** Store iShares MSCI ACWI ETF (ACWI) and SPDR S&P 500 ETF (SPY) prices at the moment each portfolio first invested, and revalue everything at the close, for example in the 18:47 run without trading. That would make the benchmark comparison fair. This week MAIN is either 4.6 points behind or level, and HIGH_RISK either 3.7 points behind or 0.8 points ahead, depending on the starting point chosen.
7. **Cut the overlap between the portfolios, or accept it on purpose.** Four shared names make the two experiments less informative. If HIGH_RISK is meant to show what aggressive sizing does, the overlap is fine. If it is meant to test a different selection style, consider a penalty for names already held in the other book.
